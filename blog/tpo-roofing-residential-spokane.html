TPO Roofing for Homes: What It Is and What It Costs

The membrane system behind most modern flat roofs, explained in plain English.

Fluid Roofing · Spokane & Airway Heights, WA

If you have been quoted for a flat or low-slope roof around Spokane, TPO has probably come up. Here is what it actually is, what it costs, and when it is the right call for a house rather than a warehouse.

What TPO is

TPO stands for thermoplastic polyolefin. It is a single-ply roofing membrane: large sheets of flexible material rolled out across the roof deck, with the seams heat-welded together rather than glued. That welded seam is the main reason it caught on. Done properly, the seam is effectively as strong as the sheet itself.

It is usually white, which matters more than it sounds. A reflective roof surface stays cooler in summer, which is easier on the membrane and on the rooms underneath it.

Why it shows up on houses, not just commercial buildings

TPO got its start on commercial flat roofs, which is why most homeowners have never heard of it. But plenty of Spokane homes have low-slope sections: a flat-roofed addition, a garage, a porch or carport cover, or a mid-century house that is low-slope throughout. Shingles do not belong on those. Below roughly a 2:12 pitch, shingles cannot shed water reliably and manufacturers will not warranty them. A membrane system is the correct answer, and TPO is one of the main options.

What TPO costs in Spokane

Installed, a rough range is about $7 to $14 per square foot. On a small residential flat section that can land in the low thousands; across a whole low-slope house it runs higher. You can get a ballpark from the estimate tool on our homepage, but treat any online number as a starting point. The real drivers are what is underneath.

What moves the price: whether the old roof is torn off or the new system goes over it, how wet the existing insulation is, how many penetrations (vents, skylights, drains) need flashing, and how hard the roof is to get materials onto.

How long it lasts

A properly installed TPO roof generally gives 20 to 30 years. Installation quality matters more here than brand does. The welds are made on site, in real weather, by the crew on your roof, which is a long way of saying the installer matters more than the material.

Where TPO is not the answer

If your roof has real pitch to it, you want shingles or metal, not membrane - see asphalt vs. metal. If your existing flat roof is sound but weathered, a coating may get you most of the life for less money. And if the deck underneath is rotted, no membrane fixes that; the deck gets repaired first.

Straight answer: TPO is a strong choice for a residential flat or low-slope roof that needs replacing, especially if you want a reflective surface and welded seams.
