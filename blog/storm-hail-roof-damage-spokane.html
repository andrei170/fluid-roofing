Storm or Hail Damage? What to Do Next

A calm, step-by-step plan for Spokane homeowners after the wind and hail pass.

Fluid Roofing · Spokane & Airway Heights, WA

After a big windstorm or hail, the damage to your roof isn't always obvious from the driveway - but left alone, a small problem turns into a soaked ceiling fast. Here's what to do, in order.

1. Safety first

Stay off the roof. Wet or storm-damaged roofs are dangerous, and you can make damage worse by walking on it. If water is actively coming inside, move belongings out of the way and put down a bucket - then call a professional.

2. Look from the ground

Walk the perimeter of your home and look for the common signs of roof storm damage:

  • Shingles missing, lifted, curled, or lying in the yard
  • Dented or dinged gutters, vents, or flashing (a hail tell-tale)
  • Piles of shingle granules in the gutters or at the bottom of downspouts
  • Water stains on ceilings or in the attic
  • Debris or fallen branches that hit the roof

3. Document everything

Take clear photos and note the date of the storm. If you end up filing an insurance claim, that early documentation matters. Don't throw away blown-off shingles - they're evidence.

Don't wait for the next storm: Even minor-looking damage lets water under the surface, where it rots decking and feeds mold. The cheapest fix is almost always the fast one. A quick inspection now can save a major repair later.

4. Get a professional inspection

A roofer can safely get up top, find damage you can't see from the ground, and tell you honestly whether you need a repair, a replacement, or just peace of mind. We can also document the damage clearly, which helps if you decide to involve your insurance company.
